A short study · about 4 minutes

Which face looks more alike?

Thanks for stopping by! On each screen you will see three computer-generated faces and tell us which of two of them looks more like the one in the middle. There are 2 warm-up screens and then 20 screens.

The faces are renderings of synthetic 3D models generated with GNM Head (Ploumpis et al., Google, 2026; Apache License 2.0): no real person is shown.

What we record

Your choices, how long each took, your browser and the size and pixel density of your screen. No name, e-mail or IP address is stored, and no cookie is used. Your answers are saved under a random participant code, so they are anonymous.

This page also keeps a small note in your browser's local storage: your progress while you answer (so a reload does not lose it) and, after you finish, a flag saying that you have taken part. Nothing is sent to us except your answers. The page loads its font from Google Fonts and sends the answers through a Google Form, so Google receives an ordinary web request.

Taking part is voluntary

You can stop at any time by closing the tab. The anonymous answers will be used for academic research on 3D face similarity and may be published in aggregate form.

How it works

Pick the face that looks more like the reference

  • The reference face is in the middle, with a coloured border. The two other faces are on its left and right.
  • Each face is shown from three angles, one under the other: front, three-quarter and side. The rows line up, so you can compare the same angle across the faces.
  • Click or tap a face, use the buttons below the faces, or press ← / → (or 1 / 2).
  • Ignore light and shadows. All faces are rendered the same way: grey material, same lighting, same camera.
  • Go with your first impression: there are no right or wrong answers.
