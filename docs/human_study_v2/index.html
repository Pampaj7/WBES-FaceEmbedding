Face similarity study

Thank you for taking part. You will compare computer-generated faces. The images are renderings of synthetic 3D face models: no real person is shown, and no photograph of anyone is used.

The 3D faces are generated with the GNM Head model (Ploumpis et al., Google, 2026), released under the Apache License 2.0.

What you will do

After 3 practice screens you will see 64 screens. Each shows one reference face in the middle and two other faces on its left and right. You pick the one that looks more similar to the reference. It takes about 10 minutes.

What is recorded

Only your choices, how long each one took, which browser you used and the size and pixel density of your screen (window width and height, device pixel ratio). No personal data: no name, no e-mail, no IP address, no cookie. Your answers are stored under a random participant code and cannot be traced back to you, so they are anonymous. Until you finish, your answers are also kept in this browser, so that reloading the page does not lose them.

Voluntary participation

You can stop at any time by closing the tab, with no consequence. The anonymous answers will be used for academic research on 3D face similarity metrics and may be published in aggregate form.

Instructions

On every screen there are three faces side by side. The reference face is in the middle, with a coloured border. The two candidate faces are on its left and right.

Each face is shown from three angles, one under the other: from the front, at three-quarter and from the side. The rows line up, so you can compare the same angle across the three faces.

Pick the candidate that looks more similar to the reference face.

  • Tap or click on a candidate, or press 1 for the left one and 2 for the right one.
  • Ignore light and shadows. All faces are rendered in the same way: grey material, same lighting, same camera, no hair, skin tone or texture.
  • Go with your first impression. There is no right answer and no time limit, but about 5 to 10 seconds per screen is the right pace.
  • You cannot go back to a previous screen.

The first 3 screens are practice. Some later screens are easy on purpose: they are there to check that the page is being looked at.
